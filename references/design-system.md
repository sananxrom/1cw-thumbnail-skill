# Design system

## Identity and framing

Eddie: use `../assets/identity/eddie-original.png` every episode. Indian middle-aged man, swept-up voluminous wavy/curly salt-and-pepper hair, moustache and small grey goatee, dark textured Indian waistcoat over a white short-sleeved shirt. Preserve facial structure, eye/nose/mouth shape, complexion and apparent age. This description supplements the photograph; it cannot replace it.

Eddie left and guest right, large edge-cropped photographic figures. Similar head sizes, safe faces and enough torso. Do not enlarge a tight guest photo into a giant head. Crop or plausibly extend torso/clothing. Preserve the guest's source expression and clothes unless requested otherwise. Enhance small photographs carefully without redesigning the face. Eddie may adopt a new pose, accessory or expression, conservative enough to retain likeness.

## Typography and geometry

- Original PSD canvas: 1280 × 720, 16:9. Request landscape 16:9 at that size or higher; inspect dimensions before claiming an exact resolution.
- Morganite **ExtraBold** for big copy: very tall, narrow, heavy glyphs. Avoid broad block-font substitutes. Avenir Next **Bold** for supporting copy/connectors.
- Centre align lines; balance visual widths by changing sizes. Shorter supporting lines are fine. Follow the wording, not a mandatory three-line template.
- Centre the group on the entire page. It can overlap bodies, props or panel edges. Do not shift it aside merely to fit the gap between people. Optional offsets depend on the words.
- Baselines rise rightward approximately 6.5°, with vertical shear and upright stems, not italics or simple rotation.
- Tracking +10 Photoshop units = 0.01em, not 10 pixels.
- Slightly generous vertical spacing: recent prompts requested roughly 20–26px between slanted glyph edges at 1280 × 720 scale. This is calibration, not a rigid measurement. Avoid touching lines or disconnected islands.
- Generous overall size and vertical spread, often somewhat high. Do not confine type to the panel or push it all down.
- White and bright yellow gently grading to golden orange. Colour follows emphasis; the last line need not always be yellow. Preserve approved punctuation and row breaks.
- Soft low-opacity down-right shadow; avoid thick outlines, heavy extrusion or all-around harsh shadows.

Image generation approximates font appearance. Actual font files are needed for exact later typesetting; no fonts are bundled or required for the initial image render.

## Scene and finish

Simple premium topic-appropriate environment. A believable lab, quantum room or institutional setting should have a few broad cues and depth, rather than an empty gradient or a mass of micro-objects. Topic can justify space, an airfield or another setting; do not force every episode into a lab.

Darken/soften background with an inward edge vignette. Keep people and type bright. A requested bright lab retains white/daylight character while edges/detail stay subdued. Palette varies by topic: teal, blue, amber/gold, muted green.

Use a faded near-black diagonal panel. Text may exceed its bounds. Sparse faint golden foreground dust, mainly lower half and across clothes, can unify the collage. It is optional, not a mandatory shower of sparks. Keep faces and words clear.

Natural photographic skin with smooth lighting transitions, subtle pores/age lines and clean hair. Avoid swirls, mottling, embossed/etched detail, waxy smoothing and extreme sharpening. Restrained rim lighting integrates subjects without neon outlines.

## Concept selection

The strongest originals integrate topic into Eddie's pose/accessories: robotic arm with a reaction to thought control; XR goggles and a reaching hand. Find an equally concrete connection for the episode without recycling unrelated props. Prefer one clear visual story cue. The guest supplies recognisable authority, not a newly invented character.

Copy is short, immediately impactful and slightly clickbaity with respect. Do not strengthen the episode's claims. Supplied bios/descriptions are creative context, not independent verification. Use questions or future language for proposed milestones.
